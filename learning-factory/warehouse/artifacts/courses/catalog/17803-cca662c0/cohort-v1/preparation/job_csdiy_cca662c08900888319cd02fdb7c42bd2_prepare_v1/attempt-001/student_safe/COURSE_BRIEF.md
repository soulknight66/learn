## Why this unit comes first

Algorithm work often ends with a sentence such as “the new version is faster.” Empirical software engineering asks what was measured, what counts as one observation, what comparison is justified, what uncertainty or heterogeneity was hidden, and whether another person can reproduce the analysis. The same questions matter when evaluating tools, mining repositories, interviewing developers, or studying engineering organizations.

In this unit, you will turn a small paired benchmark table into a precise, reproducible, and appropriately limited claim. The emphasis is as much on data contracts, validation, testing, and provenance as on arithmetic.

## Unit outcomes

By the end of this kickoff, you should be able to:

- state an empirical question, observational unit, variables, comparison, and estimand precisely;
- explain why a matched comparison differs from treating all rows as independent;
- implement a deterministic analysis command that rejects invalid inputs;
- distinguish observed evidence from causal or population-level conclusions;
- describe how benchmark design and software provenance affect credibility.
